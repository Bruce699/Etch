Motion and adaptation

Eleven later product demonstrations cover capture, inbox insertion, labeled connections, subcanvas creation, external threads, generation, source reveal, duplication, inline insertion, reordering and a second framing. Their corresponding application states run on real input. The early alpha contributes free movement and note editing. Three recordings preserve the discarded automatic-drafting and manual-boundary experiments. The motion audit retains all fifteen for replay.

  • Subcanvas creation. The staged clip drags a newly formed preview from the bottom dock. The app names a child canvas in a dialog, places a live preview and allows it to be dragged afterward. Its cards keep their shared identities and independent positions.
  • Camera and timing. Panning, preview magnification, selection, drag feedback, contextual controls, insertion guides, drawer entry and generation feedback are implemented. Camera endpoints and easing follow live measurements. Exact animation curves, scripted pointer trajectories and staged generation time have not been matched frame by frame.
  • Personalization. Local word overlap and graph edges rank suggestions. Previous labels, prompts and outline shapes can be reused. A learned personal writing model and longitudinal evaluation remain unimplemented. The closing slides propose these capabilities without specifying a training mechanism or providing training data.
  • Source fidelity. The seeded “OpenAI: The New Moat” document preserves the four passages shown in the source. Its passage-to-note mapping is hand-authored and includes inferred links where the demonstration did not show a reveal. New outlines receive their own source IDs from the model, validated against the selected notes. Valid IDs do not establish that every generated claim is supported.
  • Workspace and media. The seed reconciles different moments in Rachel’s journey into five navigable canvases. Some placements and note details were inferred. Attachments and explicit source URLs persist locally. The writer receives note text and source URLs. Automatic webpage ingestion and image recognition are outside this implementation. Original streaming media is included in the audit.
  • Platform. The staged product is desktop-first. The reconstruction adds compact layouts, keyboard entry, file export and local storage. Mobile verification covered the compact inbox and canvas controls. Touch interaction across physical devices remains unverified.

Verification

The Chrome journey and Python storage checks ran against fresh disposable databases. The writing test exercised the installed Qwen model, traced sources, edited the document, created a branch and downloaded Markdown. A separate run forced the model offline and verified the identified extractive fallback. The storage suite also verified process restart, stale-write rejection and invalid input handling.

The initial workspace contains 20 notes, five canvases, four labeled relationships and the original example document. Working data lives in SQLite. The service and model both listen on loopback.
